## What KodaCars Does (Core Concept)

KodaCars solves a three-sided problem at airports:

| Problem | Party | KodaCars Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Car sits idle for days/weeks at airport | **Asset Owner** | List your car; lot staff manage the handoff. Hands-off income. |
| Yield per parking stall is capped | **Lot Owner** | P2P revenue per stall with zero capex. Higher yield. |
| Rental counter lines, fees, upsells | **Traveler** | Grab a car from the parking lot. No lines, no wait, no upsell. |

**Key differentiation vs Turo (street P2P):** All transactions happen inside gated, monitored, 24/7 staffed airport parking. No street pickup, no lockboxes, no curbside friction. Platform-enforced photo/video at handoff. Dual GPS + OBD telematics.

## Competitive Moat vs Market

| Feature | KodaCars | Turo (P2P leader) | Enterprise / Hertz (Legacy) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset acquisition | Zero upfront — API capture via parking partners | High digital ad spend per user | Billions in depreciating fleet |
| Airport logistics | Frictionless — lot staff manage keys | High friction — curbside meetups / lockboxes | Counter lines + shuttle bottlenecks |
| Risk environment | Gated, 24/7 staffed, mandatory telematics/photos | Open street — high theft/fraud exposure | Secure but burdened by fleet depreciation |
| Insurance | Captive model — high underwriting margins | High commercial premiums (open-street risk) | Self-insured, high corporate overhead |
| Target audience | 100% airport travelers — verified flight itineraries | Broad mix — locals, tourists, daily renters | Broad travel market; rigid corporate contracts |
| Insurance cost | **$30/day target** | $30–60/day street | N/A (self-insured) |

**Previous airport P2P that failed:** FlightCar, Avail — died under $80/day insurance cost. KodaCars' captive model targets ~2% claims (vs 5–8% legacy P2P) through gated lots, vetted renters, telematics, photo checklists.

## Key Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Why It Matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Insurance economics | Prior airport P2P died at $25–80/day insurance | Captive underwriting; tight controls targeting ~2% claims |
| Captive/regulatory delays | Could slow rollout or raise capital needs | Phased rollout; backup reinsurance options |
| Operational execution | Inconsistent lot processes → claims, brand damage | App-driven SOPs (photo/video at handoff); staff certification |
| Adverse selection/fraud | Bad vehicles/actors skew losses | Bilateral vetting: verified parking customers as listers; 30+ age, clean MVR for renters |
| Channel dependence | Over-reliance on booking platforms | Revenue-share alignment; build direct/loyalty channel over time |
| Competitive response | Incumbents copy once captive economics proven | Moat in execution: captive data, bilateral trust, lot SOPs |

## Learnings & Strategic Context

1. **KodaCars is not a traditional ad-spend account** — The GTM moat is structural (gated lots, captive insurance, partner API). Marketing amplifies the moat; it doesn't replace it.
2. **Airport P2P has failed before** (FlightCar, Avail) — The key failure mode was insurance economics. KodaCars' captive model is the critical differentiator. All messaging should reinforce this.
3. **Two-sided marketplace = two messaging tracks** — Supply (car owners: "earn while you fly") and demand (travelers: "no lines, your own car from the lot") need separate campaign strategies.
4. **Referral + partner channels are zero-CAC** — Scale these before paid channels. Proven P2P growth mechanic.
5. **Year 1 is a loss** ($1.9M) — This is runway / network build. Do not expect ROAS metrics in Year 1. Success metric is lot partnerships closed and supply listings generated.
