# e-Sprinto: B2C Dealership Recruitment Meta Ad Copy
**Date:** 28 July 2026 | **Objective:** Generate dealership leads (Delhi NCR) | **CTA:** Apply for Dealership | **Destination:** Meta Instant Form

**Character limits enforced (per client feedback):** Primary Text <=125 characters, Headline <=40 characters, Description <=30 characters. Applies to English AND Hindi equally.

**Brief locked from client:**
- UPDATE: Client has asked that the ₹25 Lakhs figure not be mentioned anywhere in ad copy. All Concept 1 lines have been rewritten to reference "affordable entry" / "low entry cost" generically instead of stating the amount.
- Entry cost is referenced only in generic terms (no rupee figure disclosed)
- Support: "Full brand support," kept vague, no specifics
- No exclusive territories claimed
- No B2C sales/dealer proof exists yet; trust angle instead leans on **real, existing B2B fleet/delivery-rider trust** (per Creative 2 & 3 references)
- ROI referenced in general aspirational terms only, no fabricated %
- Competitor names (Ather, Bajaj, Ola, Okinawa, etc.) kept out of ad copy; used only to inform positioning
- No specific company stats available; only safe general EV-industry framing used where needed
- Geography: Delhi NCR
- Concept 5 uses Delhi's notified policy ending new petrol two-wheeler registrations from 2028 (client-confirmed as a confirmed/notified policy, not a draft) as the urgency driver. Recommend keeping the government notification/source on file in case Meta's ad review requests substantiation for this claim.
- Concept 2 is built around the new "EV Dealerships Opening for Delhi & Delhi NCR" creative. The on-image badge claims "#1 player in B2B EV," per client instruction the copy text (this sheet) hedges that to "a leading B2B EV brand" rather than asserting the ranking directly, since unsubstantiated ranking/superiority claims are commonly challenged under India's ASCI advertising code. The CTA for this concept only is "Apply Now" (matching the creative's button), all other concepts use "Apply for Dealership."
- Hindi copy is written in pure Hindi (Devanagari script), per client feedback reverting from the earlier Hinglish version. Brand name, acronyms (EV, NCR, B2C) and numbers are kept in Latin script as is standard practice; everything else is Devanagari. Fits the same character limits.

**Compliance flag:** Avoided absolute/guarantee language ("guaranteed returns," "no risk," "double your money") since Meta frequently rejects investment/business-opportunity ads carrying those claims. Recommend keeping this phrasing discipline in the final creative too.

## Notes for the media buyer
- All five concepts avoid naming competitor brands and avoid absolute claims ("guaranteed," "no risk," "double your investment") to reduce risk of Meta's business/investment ad review flags.
- Concept 3 and 4 both lean on the fleet-trust proof point, so I'd recommend A/B testing the corporate framing (Concept 3) against the emotional/vernacular framing (Concept 4) independently rather than assuming one will outperform the other, since they're targeting the same trust angle from different emotional registers.
- Concept 5 makes a factual regulatory claim (Delhi's 2028 petrol two-wheeler registration ban). Since it's a legal/policy statement rather than marketing opinion, keep the government notification on hand as substantiation in case Meta's ad review flags it, and recheck the date closer to launch in case the policy timeline shifts.
- Concept 2's on-image badge states "#1 player in B2B EV." That's a ranking/superiority claim, only substantiate this on the creative if e-Sprinto has verifiable data behind it (market share, fleet volume, city coverage). The copy text in this sheet deliberately hedges to "a leading B2B EV brand" rather than repeating the ranking claim in writing.
- Concept 2 uses "Apply Now" as its CTA (matching its creative's button text); all other concepts use "Apply for Dealership." Keep this distinction when setting up campaigns so each ad's CTA button matches its own creative.
- Every Primary Text, Headline, and Description across both languages has been validated to sit at or under the 125/40/30 character limits (char counts shown inline above).
