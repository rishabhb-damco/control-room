Wellspring Therapeutic Partners — Lead Management

From weekly spreadsheet to a real CRM

Wellspring's entire lead pipeline currently lives in a Google Sheet that adds one new tab every single week. This document analyzes exactly how that sheet works today — using the live data behind it — and designs the CRM that should replace it: architecture, wireframes, data model, workflow, and a phased build plan.

This isn't a template CRM pitch. Every finding below is pulled directly from the live consultation sheet — real headers, real rows, real gaps — so the design solves the problems Wellspring's team actually has, not a generic mental‑health‑practice checklist.

What's conspicuously absent: no Name, Phone, or Email column, and no structured Insurance field. Staff clearly keep identity and clinical records in a separate system of record (likely their EHR/practice‑management tool) and use Client ID as the join key. That's the right instinct — and it's the design constraint this CRM has to respect (see Compliance & Security).

Findings · 01

Biggest pain points

Ranked by how much staff time and reporting accuracy they cost every week.

1

There is no "this month" or "this quarter" — only 32 separate weeks

Every rollup (the March 2026 benchmark reference, the QBR numbers, any trend line) has to be built by hand, tab by tab. There's no live way to ask "how are we doing this quarter?" without opening dozens of sheets.

Evidence: 32 tabs from 12/8/2025 to 7/11/2026, one new tab appended every week with no linking structure between them.
2

Referral source requires a script to interpret, not a click to select

"How did you hear about Wellspring?" is free text that mixes channel labels with people's first names. A separate 8‑rule Python classifier (already built and living in lead_quality_skill.md) exists purely to turn this column into a reportable "Source" after the fact.

Evidence: values seen in one week alone — "Returning Patient", "A friend", "Denise", "Lisa", "Student", "Pediatrician", "Insurance Company", "Google", "online search", "-", "Other".
3

"Did they schedule?" is being asked to do a pipeline's job

Yes / No / Pending / blank is a flattened stand‑in for a real pipeline stage. There's no way to see, at a glance, who's mid‑follow‑up versus who's gone cold versus who genuinely said no.

Evidence: same column holds "Yes", "No", "Pending", and empty string across adjacent rows in the same week, with no documented distinction between "Pending" and blank.
4

Follow‑up activity is a sentence, not a log

All contact attempts for a lead are jammed into one free‑text cell. There's no way to count touches, measure response time, or trigger a reminder when a lead has gone quiet.

Evidence: "LVM and emailed 7/10/2026" — two actions, one date, one cell, no way to query "how many leads got 2+ follow‑ups."
5

Insurance eligibility is discovered after the fact, not checked up front

Insurance never appears as its own field — it only shows up buried in "Reason Not Scheduled" text like "Medicaid" or "Priority Medicaid," after staff have already spent time on the lead. This is almost certainly why HAP's acceptance status has stayed an unresolved question for three‑plus months in the account history.

Evidence: rows reading Reason Not Scheduled: "Medicaid" and "Priority Medicaid" — insurance as an outcome, never as an input.
6

Out‑of‑area leads aren't filtered before they consume staff time

Leads from California and London appear in the same funnel as Troy/Clinton Township prospects, discovered only when a staff member manually writes "Lives in California" as the rejection reason.

Evidence: Reason Not Scheduled: "Lives in California", "Lives in London" — no zip/geo gate exists ahead of the consult.
7

Two fields try to capture "how" and disagree with each other

Source (Internal/External/Other/Google Ads) and How did you hear are meant to reconcile but sometimes don't — a lead whose "hear" text says "Google" is sometimes tagged Source: Other. Nothing forces consistency at entry time.

8

"Type of Consultation" conflates two different questions

Contact channel (phone call vs. web form) and completion state (started vs. finished) are forced into one enum. A lead who called and completed intake has no way to be tagged as both.

Findings · 03

Data quality issues

Free‑text everywhere a controlled list belongs. Referral source, services requested, and reasons seeking treatment are all typed by hand — leading to typos ("autisim"), inconsistent phrasing, and a downstream classifier script just to make the data reportable.
Inconsistent null semantics. "-", blank, "Pending", and "Not Applicable" are used interchangeably across different columns to mean "no data," "not yet," and "n/a" — with no documented rule for which means what.
No enforced required fields. Rows exist with almost nothing filled in beyond a lead number and a date — a lead that started an inquiry and was never followed up on far enough to even get a Client ID.
No de‑duplication. With no phone/email/DOB match check, a returning patient inquiring again shows up as a brand‑new lead unless a staff member happens to write "Returning Patient" in the source field.
No validation on structured‑looking fields. Zip code and DOB are free‑entry text fields, so "-" and blank both appear where a real date picker or zip validator would catch the gap at entry.
Quasi‑PHI in an unrestricted sheet. DOB + zip + free‑text clinical detail ("Anxiety, depression, autisim") sits in a Google Sheet with no field‑level access control or audit trail. See Compliance & Security.

Blueprint · 04

Pipeline stages & workflow

Replaces the single "Did they schedule?" column with six explicit stages, each with a defined exit condition.

New

Inquiry captured, not yet reviewed

→
Contacted

Eligibility checked, ≥1 outreach attempt logged

→
Scheduled

Consultation booked

→
Attended

Consultation occurred

→
Converted

Paperwork + intake complete → active patient

→
Lost

Exit reason required: insurance / no‑response / cost / capacity / out‑of‑area / other

Daily staff workflow: open Dashboard → clear overdue‑follow‑up alerts → work the Pipeline board left‑to‑right → log every touch as an Activity (never a sentence in a spreadsheet cell) → move cards as stage changes → the system, not the staff member, computes scheduling rate, time‑to‑schedule, and source‑level quality.

Weekly manager workflow: Dashboard trend view replaces manually reading old tabs → Reports → run "Weekly Client Report" template → export → done. No spreadsheet formulas to re‑check.

Plan · 04

Compliance & security

The current sheet already stores quasi‑PHI without the controls that should come with it. Full date of birth, zip code, and free‑text mental‑health detail ("Anxiety, depression, autisim", "Self harm, depression") sit in a Google Sheet reachable by anyone with the link, with no field‑level permissions and no audit trail of who viewed or edited what.

Keep this system PHI‑light on purpose. Store client_ref only; leave clinical notes, full names, and contact details in the existing EHR/practice‑management system of record.
Host under a signed BAA. Whatever platform this is built on needs a Business Associate Agreement in place before any lead data with health‑adjacent fields moves into it.
Role‑based access from day one on anything beyond MVP — staff should not default to seeing every lead in the practice.
Audit log every field change — who touched insurance status, who moved a stage, who edited a note — the exact accountability the current sheet has none of.
