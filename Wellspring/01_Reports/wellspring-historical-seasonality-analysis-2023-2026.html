Top 2 Insights
  1. Scheduling rate has climbed every era, not just this year. 21.6% (2023) → 43.5% (2024) → 53.9% (2025+2026) — a genuine multi-year quality improvement, separate from the ramp-up seen within 2026 itself.

Seasonal Observations
October rebound (2023 & 2024): 2023's Oct (140) is its highest month, up from Aug/Sep (84 each); 2024's Oct (90) rebounds from an Aug/Sep trough (66, 71). Worth testing a modest October 2026 spend increase.
Summer softening (both years): Jun–Aug sits ~40% below each year's peak. In 2026 this coincided with scheduling rate holding 60%+ — quality didn't drop, only quantity. Maintain rather than cut summer spend.
December disagreement: lowest month of 2024 (59) but mid-pack in 2023 (112). The two years disagree — don't pre-emptively cut December 2026 budget on this alone.

The Funnel: Where Leads Are Actually Lost

2025+2026 is the only era with paperwork/intake tracking
Top 2 Insights
  1. The scheduling rate has more than doubled since 2023 and kept climbing: 21.6% → 43.5% → 53.9%. That's the one funnel stage tracked in all three eras — every other stage below it (paperwork, intake) simply didn't exist as a measurement before 2025, so we have no way to know if today's 35% paperwork drop-off is new or if it's always been there, hidden.
  2. We only gained the ability to see the paperwork/intake stages in the current era — and what we found (a 35% drop-off between scheduling and paperwork) was invisible in 2023 and 2024 by definition, not because it didn't exist. This is a case where the year-over-year comparison itself is the finding: better tracking revealed a leak that likely existed all along.

Channel Performance: Volume vs Quality

Feb–Aug 2026 only — the only period with Source tracking
Top 2 Insights
  1. Psychology Today is the only channel that's shown up as a top performer in every era we have data for — 38 leads in 2023, 163 in 2024, and still running strong in 2025+2026 (34 leads, 68.8% scheduling) — yet it's the one channel that's LOST visibility over time. It was an explicitly tracked, named source in 2023/2024; today it's uncounted, buried inside the generic "Other" bucket.
  2. The entire channel-tracking taxonomy changed character between eras, and we lost a whole category of insight as a result. 2023/2024 tracked relationship-based referrals — doctors' offices (142 leads in 2024 alone) and existing/returning patients (224 combined) were major, named channels. The current Source field only tracks ad-platform categories (Google/Meta/Internal/Other/External), so we can no longer tell whether physician and patient referrals are still a major engine or have quietly declined — the comparison itself is the gap.

Capture Method & Service Mix

Service mix compared 2023 vs 2024 vs 2025+2026 · capture method is 2025+2026 only
Top 2 Insights
  1. Psychotherapy/Individual Therapy's share of inbound has climbed every single era: 47.1% (2023) → 51.2% (2024) → 61.9% (2025+2026). This is a consistent multi-year shift, not a one-off — the practice has organically become more psychotherapy-dominant over time.
  2. Medication Management's share moved the opposite way — and not in a straight line: 36.1% (2023) → 42.2% (2024) → 23.0% (2025+2026). It grew before falling, so this isn't simple decline — worth checking with Wellspring whether it's falling demand or a capacity/NP-availability constraint on the supply side.

Who's Actually Inquiring

Demographics only exist for 2025+2026 — 2023 has no DOB/sex field; 2024's DOB is filled for just 0.7% of records
Top 2 Insights
  1. Sunday is consistently the weakest day for scheduling across all three eras — but Saturday isn't, and that only shows up by comparing years. Sunday: 14.3% (2023) → 32.1% (2024) → 43.9% (2025+2026), weak every time. Saturday: 17.6% (2023) → 46.4% (2024) → 31.6% (2025+2026) — bad in two years but genuinely fine in 2024. A single-year read would have wrongly written off Saturday as a bad day; across three years, only Sunday earns that.
  2. Age and gender have zero historical baseline to compare against — this itself is the finding. 2023 never collected DOB or sex at all; 2024's DOB field is filled for only 7 of 1,064 records (0.7%, unusable). The 27% children/teen share and 73/27 gender split reported here are a first-ever baseline for Wellspring, not a trend — there's nothing to compare them to yet, and that's a tracking gap worth closing so 2026 can be a real baseline for 2027.
